Running taught me that the difference between a good day and a bad day is mostly just showing up.

There are mornings where I lace up feeling invincible and cover 6km like it's nothing. And there are mornings; most mornings, honestly, where I don't feel like it at all. Where my legs feel like lead and my brain lists every single reason why staying in bed is the rational choice.

I run anyway. Not always fast. Not always far. But I run.

And somewhere around the 10-minute mark, something shifts. The resistance dissolves. It's not that running becomes easy, it doesn't, but the *wanting to quit* noise in my head goes quiet.

I've come to believe this is the real lesson running teaches. Not discipline. Not fitness. It's that the hardest part of almost anything is the first ten minutes. That the voice saying *"I can't"* or *"not today"* is almost always lying.

Most things in life have that 10-minute problem. The hard email you've been avoiding. The project you haven't started. The conversation that needs to happen.

Start anyway. Give it 10 minutes.

The rest usually follows.
